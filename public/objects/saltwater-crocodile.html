    About the saltwater crocodile

    The saltwater crocodile is the largest living reptile and ranges from India and Sri Lanka through Southeast Asia to northern Australia and the Solomon Islands. Males grow far larger than females: the Northern Territory Government says males normally reach up to 6 m while females normally grow to a little over 3 m. San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance gives an average length of 16 to 20 ft and a weight of about 2,000 lb.

    • Large males can exceed six metres in length and weigh more than 1,000 kg, while females are usually around three metres long. [3]
    • Crocodiles of up to 10 m were recorded in the wild in the past, but such sizes are extremely rare. [2]
    • An average hatchling weighs 72 g and is 29.3 cm long; females mature at around 2.3 m and males at around 3.3 m. [2]
    • It is one of the most widely distributed crocodilians, found from southern India and Sri Lanka east to Micronesia and south to northern Australia. [4]

    Notes on these figures

    • San Diego Zoo's 16 to 20 ft 'average' and 2,000 lb weight describe large adults, not the species as a whole; females are much smaller.
    • Queensland Museum gives a slightly different maximum ('up to 7 metres but rarely more than 5 metres') from the Northern Territory Government's 'males normally up to 6 metres'.
    • The IUCN Crocodile Specialist Group account gives lengths but no weights.
