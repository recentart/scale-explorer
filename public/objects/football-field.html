    About the American football field

    An NFL field is a rectangle 360 feet long and 160 feet wide. A 10-yard end zone at each end leaves 300 feet of field of play between the goal lines. The crossbar at each end sits over the end line, 10 feet above the ground, on a single-post goal set back behind it, and the uprights rise another 35 feet above the crossbar.

    • The crossbar is 18 feet 6 inches long, and the uprights are 3 to 4 inches in diameter. [1]
    • NFL goal posts are the single-standard type, bright gold, offset from the end line, and have a 4-by-42-inch orange ribbon attached to the top of each upright. [1]
    • The inbounds lines (hash marks) are 70 feet 9 inches in from each sideline. [1]
    • The web version of the NFL rulebook on the league's Football Operations site gives the same 360 ft by 160 ft field and the same goal-post dimensions. [2]

    Notes on these figures

    • The rulebook gives the overall length and width in feet (360 ft by 160 ft), not in yards; 120 yd and 53 1/3 yd are the same values converted.
    • The 300 ft field-of-play length comes from the dimension labels on the rulebook's field diagram. The rule text itself sets the goal lines 10 yards in from each end line.
    • Both sources are published by the NFL, the body that sets these dimensions, so there is no more authoritative independent source to check against.
