    About Angel Falls

    Angel Falls, also known as Kerepakupai Merú, is the world's tallest waterfall, in Canaima National Park in Bolívar state, southeastern Venezuela. It pours off the flat-topped Auyán-tepui and falls a total of 979 m (3,212 ft), including a single uninterrupted drop of 807 m (2,648 ft). The falls were named after the American pilot Jimmie Angel, who flew over them in the 1930s.

    • The falls are on a branch of the Carrao River, an upper tributary of the Caroní, and are best seen from the air because of the dense jungle around them. [2] [1]
    • The height was last surveyed by a National Geographic expedition in 1949. Guinness notes that if only the 807 m drop counted, the 948 m multi-drop Tugela Falls in South Africa would be the tallest waterfall overall. [2]
    • The Pemón name Kerepakupai Merú means 'waterfall of the deepest place'. In 2009 Venezuela's president declared that this Indigenous name should be used. [2] [1]
    • Canaima National Park covers about 3 million hectares, and roughly 65% of it is table mountain (tepui) formations. [3]

    Notes on these figures

    • No Venezuelan park authority page could be found; UNESCO's Canaima page gives only a rounded 1,000 m for the world's highest waterfall, so the 979 m and 807 m figures come from Britannica and Guinness World Records.
    • The 979 m total includes cascades below the main plunge; some question whether all of it should count as one waterfall.
    • Britannica says Jimmie Angel crash-landed nearby in 1937, while Guinness says he recorded the falls after flying over them in November 1933.
    • The waterfall outline is illustrative; only its height is to scale.
