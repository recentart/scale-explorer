    About the Colosseum

    The Colosseum is the oval amphitheatre just east of the Palatine Hill in Rome, begun under the emperor Vespasian around 70-72 CE on the site of the drained lake of Nero's Golden House. Titus dedicated it in 80 CE with 100 days of games, and Domitian finished the top storey in 82 CE. Built of travertine, tufa and concrete, it measures about 189 by 156 m, stands about 48 m high and held around 50,000 spectators.

    • It is the largest amphitheatre in the world. [3]
    • Unlike earlier amphitheatres, which were mostly dug into hillsides, it is freestanding, held up by barrel and groin vaults. [2]
    • Its outer arcades are framed by Doric, Ionic and Corinthian half-columns, and a huge awning called the velarium, worked by sailors, shaded the crowd. [2]
    • The arena floor was a large wooden platform covered with sand. [1]
