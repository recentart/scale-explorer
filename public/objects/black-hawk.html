    About the Black Hawk helicopter

    The Sikorsky UH-60 Black Hawk is the U.S. Army's main utility helicopter, used for air assault, medical evacuation and command roles; it first flew in 1974 and more than 5,000 have been built. The Army's operator's manual gives an overall length of 64 ft 10 in with the rotors turning and a main rotor diameter of 53 ft 8 in. With the rotors and tail pylon folded for storage it shrinks to 41 ft 4 in long.

    • The fuselage is 7 ft 9 in wide; with external fuel tanks on the stub wings the helicopter spans 21 ft. [1]
    • A Black Hawk carries 11 troops, and the UH-60M's cargo hook can lift 9,000 lb. [2]
    • The Black Hawk first flew on October 17, 1974, and over 5,000 have been delivered, making it Sikorsky's longest production program. [3]

    Notes on these figures

    • Dimensions come from the Army operator's manual for the UH-60A and UH-60L (hosted on the Internet Archive, distribution statement A: approved for public release). No primary source giving UH-60M overall dimensions was found; Lockheed Martin's product page and UH-60M brochure give weights only.
    • Lengths are stored in decimal feet converted from the manual's feet-and-inches (for example 64 ft 10 in = 64.83 ft).
    • The manual's height figure is to the top of the tail rotor's path; the fixed structure and main rotor hub are lower.
