    About the Andromeda Galaxy

    The Andromeda Galaxy (M31) is the nearest large spiral galaxy to the Milky Way, about 2.5 million light-years away. NASA estimates it at roughly 220,000 to 260,000 light-years across, well over twice the commonly quoted width of our own galaxy's disc. From a dark site it is visible to the unaided eye, and its angular size is about six times that of the full Moon.

    • At about 2.5 million light-years away, Andromeda is the Milky Way's largest galactic neighbour; in NASA's Galaxy Evolution Explorer ultraviolet image the entire galaxy spans 260,000 light-years. [2]
    • NASA Space Place gives the disc of Andromeda as about 260,000 light-years, compared with about 100,000 light-years for the Milky Way's disc. [3]
    • In angular size Andromeda appears about six times the apparent diameter of the full Moon; a 2025 Hubble panorama of it needed over 600 overlapping snapshots. [4]
