    About the General Sherman tree

    The General Sherman Tree is a giant sequoia in the Giant Forest grove of Sequoia National Park, California. The National Park Service calls it the largest tree in the world by volume, estimating its wood at about 52,500 cubic feet. It stands 274.9 ft (83.8 m) above its base and measures 102.6 ft (31.1 m) around at the ground.

    • The trunk is 17.5 ft (5.3 m) across at 60 ft above the base and 14.0 ft (4.3 m) across at 180 ft above the base. [1]
    • The first large branch starts 130.0 ft (39.6 m) above the base, and the largest branch is 6.8 ft (2.1 m) in diameter. [1]
    • In lumber terms the tree probably holds about 630,000 board feet of wood. [1]
    • The world's second-largest tree, the General Grant Tree, stands in the Grant Grove area of Kings Canyon National Park. [1]

    Notes on these figures

    • 'Largest' refers to volume of wood; taller trees exist, including coast redwoods.
    • The park does not say which ton it means for the trunk weight; short tons (2,000 lb) are assumed because the page uses US customary units. The figure covers the trunk only.
    • The park's statistics table gives no measurement date, and the tree's dimensions change slowly as it grows and loses branches.
    • The tree outline is illustrative; only its height is to scale.
