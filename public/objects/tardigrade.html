    About the tardigrade

    Tardigrades, or water bears, are tiny eight-legged invertebrates that live in damp moss, sand, fresh water and the sea around the world. Adults of different species span roughly 50 to 2,100 micrometres in length, and most are about a millimetre or smaller. They are known for surviving drying out and other extreme conditions, which has made them a subject of space research.

    • About 1,200 tardigrade species are known according to this review, and the true number is thought to be much higher. [1]
    • The body has a head segment followed by four segments, each carrying one pair of unjointed legs that usually end in claws. [1]
    • Adult tardigrades live roughly two to several months. [1]
    • Tardigrades live in damp moss, on flowering plants, in sand, in fresh water and in the sea. [2]
