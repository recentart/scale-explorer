    About the Golden Gate Bridge

    The Golden Gate Bridge is a suspension bridge carrying U.S. Route 101 and California State Route 1 across the Golden Gate strait between San Francisco and Marin County. Built under chief engineer Joseph Strauss from January 1933, it opened to vehicles on 28 May 1937 as the tallest and longest suspension bridge in the world. Its two towers stand 746 ft above the water and are 4,200 ft apart, and with its approaches the bridge runs 8,981 ft (1.7 miles) from abutment to abutment.

    • Each of the two main cables is 36 3/8 in (0.92 m) thick including its outer wrapping. [1]
    • The two main towers together weigh 44,000 tons (40,200,000 kg). [1]
    • It held the record for the world's longest main span until the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge in New York was completed in 1964. [2]
    • A movable safety net beneath the work saved 19 men during construction, but 11 workers died over the four years of building. [2]
