    About the US penny

    The one-cent coin, or penny, was one of the first coins made by the U.S. Mint after 1792 and shows President Lincoln on the front. Today's penny is copper-plated zinc, 19.05 mm across and 1.52 mm thick, and weighs 2.50 g. The Mint struck its final circulating penny on November 12, 2025, but the coin remains legal tender.

    • The penny is 97.5% zinc and 2.5% copper, with a plain edge. [1]
    • The final circulating penny was struck in Philadelphia on November 12, 2025, ending a 232-year production run. [2]
    • The cost of making each penny rose from 1.42 cents to 3.69 cents over the preceding decade, and an estimated 300 billion pennies remain in circulation. [2]
