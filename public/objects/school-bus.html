    About the school bus

    The familiar yellow school bus in the United States is usually a Type C, or conventional, bus: the engine sits under a hood in front of the windshield and the entrance door is behind the front wheels. In a study funded through the US DOT university transportation centers program, a 65/66-passenger Type C bus had a manufacturer-specified length of about 35.6 ft (10.9 m), and today's national standards cap school buses at 45 ft. Blue Bird lists its current Vision model as 96 inches wide and 121 to 128 inches tall.

    • The Blue Bird Vision seats up to 83 passengers in its diesel, propane and gasoline versions. [2]
    • North Carolina's Type C specification limits body width to 96 inches and lists approximate body lengths of 244 to 402 inches for 41- to 72-passenger buses. [3]

    Notes on these figures

    • School bus lengths vary with seating capacity. The 35.6 ft typical length is one manufacturer-specified 65/66-passenger bus from a 1998 study; the largest buses approach the 45 ft limit.
    • Current manufacturer specification sheets (Blue Bird, IC Bus, Thomas Built) list wheelbases, height and width but not overall length.
