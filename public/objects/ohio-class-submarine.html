    About the Ohio-class submarine

    The Ohio class is the US Navy's fleet of nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarines, often called 'boomers', built by General Dynamics Electric Boat. The Navy lists each boat at 560 ft (170.69 m) long with a 42 ft beam, displacing 18,750 tons when submerged. The Navy lists 14 boats, each now carrying up to 20 Trident II D5 missiles, and the Columbia class is planned to replace them.

    • There are 14 Ohio-class ballistic missile submarines, based at Bangor, Washington, and Kings Bay, Georgia. [1]
    • Each boat originally carried up to 24 missiles; under the New START treaty four tubes on each were permanently deactivated, leaving a maximum of 20. [1]
    • Each submarine has two crews, Blue and Gold, that take turns on patrol. On average a boat spends 77 days at sea, then 35 days in port for maintenance. [1]
    • The crew is 15 officers and 144 enlisted sailors, and the top speed is given as 20+ knots. [1]
    • The replacement Columbia class is also 560 ft long, with a 43 ft beam and a displacement of 20,800 long tons. [1]

    Notes on these figures

    • navy.mil returns HTTP 403 to plain fetches, so the fact file was read in headless Chrome.
    • Displacement is the mass of water pushed aside by the hull, which equals the submarine's mass when floating. The Navy's 'tons' are long tons; the metric figures are stored.
    • The four Ohio-class boats converted to guided-missile submarines (SSGN) are not covered by this fact file.
    • No height (hull depth or sail height) was found in the Navy source, so only the length axis is drawn.
