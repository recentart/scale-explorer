    About Mount Kilimanjaro

    Kilimanjaro is a volcanic massif in northeastern Tanzania, close to the Kenyan border, and the highest point in Africa. Its central cone, Kibo, reaches 5,895 m (19,340 ft). The mountain stands on its own above the surrounding plains, and UNESCO describes it as the largest free-standing volcanic mass in the world.

    • Kilimanjaro has three main volcanic peaks: Kibo, Mawenzi and Shira. [2]
    • Kilimanjaro National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, covers some 75,575 hectares and is encircled by mountain forest. [2]
    • Europeans first learned of the mountain in 1848, when it was reached by the German missionaries Johannes Rebmann and Johann Ludwig Krapf. [1]

    Notes on these figures

    • The Tanzania National Parks page for Kilimanjaro returned a 'page not found' error, so the height comes from Britannica and UNESCO, which agree on 5,895 m.
    • The 4,877 m rise is measured from the surrounding plains, not from sea level, so it is smaller than the summit elevation.
    • The mountain outline is illustrative; only its height above sea level is to scale.
