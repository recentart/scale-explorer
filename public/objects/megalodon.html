    About the megalodon

    Megalodon was a giant shark whose earliest fossils date to about 23 million years ago. Because its skeleton was cartilage, almost nothing survives but teeth and a few vertebrae, so its size has to be estimated. Tooth-based estimates put the largest individuals at 15 to 18 metres, while a 2025 study of fossil backbones suggested it may have reached 24.3 metres and about 94 tonnes. Its teeth could be nearly 18 centimetres long.

    • No complete megalodon skeleton has ever been found; the 24.3 m estimate came from nearly complete and partial vertebral columns found in Belgium and Denmark. [1]
    • Great white shark teeth are usually about 5 cm long, compared with almost 18 cm for the largest megalodon tooth. [1]
    • Megalodon is not a close relative of the great white shark and may have had a slimmer body than a scaled-up great white. [1]
    • A 2021 method based on tooth width, published in Palaeontologia Electronica, estimated megalodon at up to 65 ft, compared with earlier estimates of about 50 to 60 ft. [2]
