    About the ostrich

    The common ostrich of Africa's grasslands and savannas is the tallest and heaviest living bird. It cannot fly but is built for running, with long, powerful two-toed legs. San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance lists adult males at 6.9 to 9 ft tall and females at 5.7 to 6.2 ft, with males weighing 220 to 287 lb.

    • Ostriches can sprint in short bursts at up to 43 mph (70 km/h) and hold a steady 31 mph (50 km/h); a single stride can cover 10 to 16 ft. [1]
    • An ostrich egg averages about 6 by 5 inches (15 by 13 cm) and weighs about 3 lb. [1]
    • Ostrich eyes are about 2 inches (5 cm) across, the largest of any bird. [2]
    • The Smithsonian's National Zoo describes ostriches as the fastest two-legged animals on Earth. [2]

    Notes on these figures

    • San Diego Zoo's metric conversion for male height contains a typo ('21. to 2.7 meters'), so the feet values (6.9 to 9 ft) are stored.
    • The Smithsonian's National Zoo gives a broader adult weight range (200 to 300 lb) than San Diego Zoo's sex-specific ranges.
