    About the Hoover Dam

    Hoover Dam is a concrete arch-gravity dam in Black Canyon on the Colorado River, on the Arizona-Nevada state line. It was built between 1931 and 1936 and holds back Lake Mead. In cross-section it tapers like an axe head, from 660 ft thick at its base to 45 ft at the top, where a road crosses the crest.

    • The dam itself contains three and one-quarter million cubic yards of concrete; the dam, powerplant and associated works together contain 4,360,000 cubic yards. [1]
    • Hoover Dam weighs more than 6,600,000 tons. [1]
    • The towers and ornaments on the parapet rise 40 feet above the crest. [1]
    • The maximum water pressure at the base of the dam is 45,000 pounds per square foot. [1]
    • As many as 20,000 vehicles a day drive across the 45 ft wide top of the dam between Nevada and Arizona. [4]
    • The final bucket of concrete was placed in the main dam on May 29, 1935. [3]

    Notes on these figures

    • Reclamation pages give different concrete totals: 3.25 million cubic yards in the dam alone and 4,360,000 cubic yards including the powerplant and associated works (FAQ); 4,400,000 cubic yards for the dam, powerplant and associated structures (history and project-data pages); and '4 1/2 million cubic yards' (Kid Facts page).
    • The weight is given only as 'more than 6,600,000 tons', with no unit stated (probably short tons), so it is recorded as a fact rather than a measure.
    • Reclamation's project-data page confirms the headline dimensions independently of the FAQ and history pages: structural height 726.4 ft, crest length 1,244 ft, crest width 45 ft and base width 660 ft.
    • Reclamation describes the dam type in three ways: 'concrete arch-gravity' (FAQ), 'concrete gravity arch' (history page) and 'concrete thick arch' (project-data page).
