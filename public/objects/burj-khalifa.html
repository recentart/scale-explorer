    About the Burj Khalifa

    Burj Khalifa in Dubai, United Arab Emirates, completed in 2010, is the world's tallest building. CTBUH measures it at 828 m to its architectural top, which includes the spire, and 829.8 m to its very tip. It has 163 floors above ground, but the highest occupied floor is at 585.4 m, far below the top of the spire.

    • Burj Khalifa has 163 floors above ground and 1 below ground. [1]
    • It was completed in 2010. [1]
    • Its observatory is 555.7 m above the ground. [1]
    • Guinness World Records lists it as the tallest building, at 828 m (2,716 ft 6 in). [2]

    Notes on these figures

    • The commonly quoted 828 m is CTBUH's height to the architectural top, which includes the spire; it is not the height to the tip. CTBUH gives 829.8 m to the tip. The task brief called 828 m the 'to tip' height, which is not correct.
    • Guinness World Records independently gives 828 m (2,716 ft 6 in), which matches CTBUH's architectural height.
    • The official site burjkhalifa.ae returned HTTP 403, both live and through the Internet Archive, so no figures were read from it.
