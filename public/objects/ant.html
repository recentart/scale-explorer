    About the black garden ant

    The black garden ant is a small brown-black ant and one of the most familiar ants in European gardens. Its workers, the non-reproductive females seen foraging for most of the year, are about 3.4 to 5 mm long. Winged queens are roughly twice that size, and a colony has a single queen with anywhere from a few hundred to thousands of workers.

    • Winged queens are almost twice the size of the workers. [1]
    • A colony has a single queen and from several hundred up to 10,000 workers. [2]
    • What was long called the common black ant is actually two separate species, Lasius niger and Lasius platythorax, first told apart in 1991. [2]
    • The ants seen outside the nest for most of the year are female workers gathering food; large nests can hold more than 20,000 workers. [3]

    Notes on these figures

    • No university or museum page giving a Lasius niger worker body length could be fetched (AntWiki was blocked by a bot check, the BWARS species account gives no size, and a 2020 body-size study measured only head width). The worker, queen and male lengths come from NatureSpot, a UK biological recording site.
    • The Natural History Museum says queens 'can be up to 15 millimetres long', well above NatureSpot's 8 to 9 mm. The NHM article does not say how that length was measured, so it is not used as a measure.
    • No source with an ant's body mass was found.
