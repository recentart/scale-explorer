    About the Statue of Liberty

    The Statue of Liberty stands in New York Harbor. Its thin copper skin is held up by an internal framework, and the figure stands on a pedestal on top of a concrete foundation. The statue alone is about 46 m from its base to the torch, and the whole monument is about 93 m from the ground to the tip of the torch.

    • The concrete foundation weighs 54,000,000 lb. [1]
    • The concrete foundation under the pedestal is 65 ft (20 m) tall and was completed in 1884. [2]
    • The copper skin is only 3/32 of an inch thick, about the thickness of two pennies. [1]
    • In wind the statue can sway up to 3 inches and the torch up to 6 inches. [1]
    • The current torch, installed in 1986, is copper covered in 24-karat gold leaf. The original torch was moved to the Torch Gallery of the Statue of Liberty Museum in 2018. [1]
    • The right arm is 42 ft long, the index finger 8 ft long, and the head measures 17 ft 3 in from chin to cranium. [1]

    Notes on these figures

    • NPS gives no single total weight for the statue. It lists the copper, the framework and the concrete foundation separately.
    • NPS lists no separate height for the pedestal alone; its 'Ground to pedestal' figure (154 ft) includes the foundation. The 89 ft pedestal height comes from the ASCE Metropolitan Section. Its 65 ft foundation plus 89 ft pedestal adds up to the NPS figure of 154 ft.
    • ASCE Metropolitan Section independently gives the same overall height (305 ft, 93 m) and statue height (151 ft, 46 m) as NPS. Its weights are much lower, though: 62,000 lb of copper and 250,000 lb of iron framework, against 176,000 lb and 440,000 lb from NPS. The NPS figures are used because NPS is the official source, but the weights should be treated with some caution.
    • NPS gives each height in both feet-and-inches and metres; the metric values are recorded here and the imperial ones are in the notes.
