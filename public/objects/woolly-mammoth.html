    About the woolly mammoth

    The woolly mammoth was an Ice Age member of the elephant family, adapted to cold climates with a thick coat of hair, small ears, a layer of fat under the skin and spiral-curved tusks. The Natural History Museum, London says adults stood about 3 to 3.5 metres at the shoulder and weighed about 4 to 6 tonnes depending on sex, roughly between the sizes of modern Asian and African elephants. Its back was high at the shoulder and sloped down towards the tail.

    • Its tusks curved in a spiral, bending in two directions, unlike the gentle one-direction curve of living elephants' tusks. [1]
    • Southern and steppe mammoths were bigger still, weighing some 10 tonnes and standing up to four metres tall. [1]
    • Under its thick skin was a layer of insulating fat up to 8 cm (3 inches) thick, beneath a woolly undercoat about 2.5 cm deep. [2]
    • Island mammoths could be tiny: the Californian pygmy mammoth stood about 1.7 metres tall and the Sardinian mammoth about 1.5 metres. [1]

    Notes on these figures

    • All sizes are estimates from fossils and frozen remains.
    • The Natural History Museum's spoken estimate 'about four to five or six tonnes' (varying with sex) is stored as a 4-6 t range.
    • Britannica's 3-3.7 m is described as how tall mammoths stood, without saying it is at the shoulder; the headline uses the Natural History Museum's explicit shoulder height.
    • The Royal BC Museum's pages were checked but gave no shoulder height or weight for the living animal (only 2 m tusks on its replica), so they were not used.
