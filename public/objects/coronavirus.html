    About the coronavirus particle

    SARS-CoV-2 is the coronavirus behind the COVID-19 pandemic. Under the electron microscope its particles look roughly round, studded with spike proteins that give the family its crown-like appearance. Early imaging measured the particles at about 60 to 140 nm across, and later cryo-electron studies of Omicron sublineages found average diameters of about 75 to 82 nm.

    • Cryo-electron tomography counted on average about 23 pre-fusion spikes on each Omicron BA.1 particle and about 35 on each BA.2 particle, compared with about 26 reported for the original virus. [2]
    • The Omicron BA.1 and BA.2 particles were found to be similar in size to the original (wild-type) virus grown in the same cells. [2]

    Notes on these figures

    • No CDC, NIH or NIAID web page stating the particle size could be fetched (NIAID returned 403 and PubMed returned a CAPTCHA), so sizes come from peer-reviewed papers hosted on NIH's PubMed Central.
    • Particles are pleomorphic (not perfectly round), and measured size depends on variant, preparation and imaging method.
    • No sourced mass or volume was found.
