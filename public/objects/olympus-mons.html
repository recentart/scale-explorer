    About Olympus Mons

    Olympus Mons is a shield volcano on Mars and the largest known volcano in the solar system. Its height is quoted from about 22 km (ESA) to 27 km (NASA), roughly two and a half to three times Mount Everest's height above sea level. It is over 600 km wide at the base, ringed by a cliff up to 6 km high, and topped by a caldera about 3 km deep.

    • NASA's Viking mosaic caption describes Olympus Mons as 27 km high and over 600 km wide at the base, surrounded by a scarp up to 6 km high. [1]
    • ESA gives Olympus Mons an average elevation of 22 km, with a summit caldera about 3 km deep. [2]
    • NASA's Mars facts page says the volcano's base covers an area as large as the state of Arizona. [3]

    Notes on these figures

    • Height depends on the reference level: ESA's 22 km is an elevation above the Mars reference surface, while NASA's Viking caption gives 27 km.
    • NASA's Mars facts page gives a much larger 'more than 25 miles (that's more than 132,000 feet, or 40 kilometers) tall from base to summit'; this is far above the other agency figures and is not used in the range.
    • The base width is stated only as 'over 600 kilometers', so 600 km is a lower bound.
